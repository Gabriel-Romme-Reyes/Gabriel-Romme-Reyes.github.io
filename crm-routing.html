CRM · DECISION & RECOVERY SYSTEMS

Customer-to-Sales Routing Engine

Within the CRM itself, I worked on deciding which operator can receive a conversation and keeping that work recoverable when processing is interrupted.

What I built

Assignment & decision visibility

Worked on eligibility and explanations of operator selection, so routing decisions can be inspected.

Durable retries & recovery

Implemented persistence and retry handling for eligible work, including idempotent replay admission and bounded recovery workflows.

Worker lifecycle

Worked on preparing replacement workers before persistence, activating them after checkpoint persistence, and handling discard, fallback, and retirement.

Result

660K assignments / 30 days · peak 175/min · 120K customers · 95 salespeople

Across a read-only 30-day production window, routing recorded 660,000 non-shadow assignments involving 120,879 customers, 95 salespeople, and 455,538 customer-to-sales relationships. Peak observed load was 175 assignments per minute, 4,509 per hour, and 46,860 per day. At the September 27 capture, the system held 2,122 active assignments. These are observed production peaks, not a tested capacity ceiling. My work covered routing, CQRS projections, worker lifecycle, and recovery within the broader team-built CRM.

  • TypeScript
  • Python
  • PostgreSQL

  • Recovery workflows
Why this work mattered

Who should receive a conversation, and what happens if processing stops?

Routing needs more than a selection rule. Eligibility can change, workers can be replaced, and interrupted work needs a way back into processing. Decisions also need to be understandable to the people operating the system.
